QA Lab

Four working demos of how I test subscription money paths

Each demo is a small, working version of a pattern from my Growth QA work: checking prices against a source of truth, minting test accounts in an exact billing state, turning a ticket into a test plan, and alerting on a funnel drop. They were built from scratch for this page on a fictional product, Lumen Notes, with invented plans, prices and data — no employer code, data or pricing. Everything runs in your browser.

The fictional product

Lumen Notes is a made-up note-taking app with three plans, monthly or yearly billing, and four markets. This price book is the source of truth the demos test against.

MarketCurrencyVATPlus / monthPlus / yearTeam / seat / monthTeam / seat / year

Prices are net. Germany, the UK and Israel display VAT-inclusive prices at checkout; the US displays net. Free has no billing.

1 · Price check

The pattern behind my pricing-validation agent: read what checkout actually charges, compare every market × plan × cycle with the price book, explain each mismatch, and post the result where people will see it. Build v2.14 has three planted bugs; v2.15 is the fix.

Pick a build and run the check. It compares 16 prices.

2 · Test-account factory

Subscription bugs depend on account state. The factory mints an account in exactly the state a scenario needs — plan, cycle, market and billing state — with dates that make sense for that state. The audit then compares what each test account should be with what billing says it is, so a drifted account never poisons a test run.

3 · Ticket → test plan

How I turn a ticket into tests: read it, find what it doesn't say, write Gherkin for both, and export cases a test-management tool can import. In real work an AI assistant drafts steps 2 and 3 and I review them; here they're scripted so the demo runs offline.

4 · Funnel alert

Production as a QA signal. The rule: alert when a market's daily checkout → paid rate falls more than 20% below its trailing 7-day average. Release v2.14 went out on Sep 11 in this fixture story; switch markets to see which one it broke.
