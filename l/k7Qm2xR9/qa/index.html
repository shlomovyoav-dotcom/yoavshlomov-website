QA at Artlist — Growth team

July 2025 – July 2026, in the R&D Growth group, as part of a QA team led by a QA Lead. The Growth team owns the money paths: the pricing page, the plan modal, checkout, upgrades, the credits wallet, and the free-trial and guest journeys into a paid plan. My job was to make sure none of that broke — and to build the tooling that made checking it faster.

  • “Yoav owned QA across our most business-critical areas — the subscription, billing, and upgrade funnels.” — Yuval Bazilevich, Growth Team Lead, Artlist
  • “Providing QA for the Growth team — covering visitor, free-trial, and upgrade journeys — and maintaining and extending our test automation.” — Vili Medina, Head of R&D, Growth, Artlist

Day to day

  • Tested every Growth release before it shipped: pricing pages, plan modal, checkout, plan changes, credits wallet and trial-to-paid, on desktop and mobile web, across currencies and VAT.
  • Turned Jira tickets and specs into test plans — Gherkin cases in CSV, imported into Zephyr Scale — and flagged gaps in the spec before development started.
  • Kept the Playwright end-to-end suites green in GitLab CI, and wrote new journey specs and page objects when a plan or flow was added.
  • Created and maintained the team's QA accounts in the exact subscription state a scenario needed.
  • Watched production — Datadog monitors, Mixpanel funnel alerts, Sentry — triaged conversion drops and owned incidents to closure.
  • Reproduced customer issues escalated from support, and filed bugs engineers could reproduce first time: account state, steps, Chrome DevTools (Console and Network) evidence.
  • Checked billing and Zephyr APIs in Postman when the UI was not enough, and ran basic SQL on Mixpanel and Zendesk extracts.
  • Tested the AI features in my area — a generative prompt assistant in the product, and the third-party AI agent answering support — for accuracy and edge cases.
  • Wrote the team's Claude and Cursor skills and published plugins to the internal skills marketplace, so the same checks ran the same way for everyone.

Things I built

  • Billing safety net

    Nightly pricing check against Chargebee

    Problem: a wrong price on the pricing page is expensive and easy to miss by eye — it usually surfaces when a customer pays it.

    What I built: a tool, written in TypeScript and scheduled in GitLab CI, that reads the live pricing page every night and compares it with the Chargebee catalog across 8 currencies, VAT-inclusive and exclusive, monthly and yearly, and posts every mismatch to Slack. I also packaged the same check as a read-only agent tool the team could ask for any plan's current price.

    Result: caught 2 billing misconfigurations before they reached customers — one of them an ILS plan off by ₪7.38 from the source of truth.

    • TypeScript
    • Chargebee API
    • GitLab CI
    • Slack

    Described in Yuval Bazilevich's letter.

  • Test data

    Entity Builder — test accounts on demand

    Problem: subscription bugs depend on account state — guest, trial, expired, upgraded, a specific plan and credit tier — and setting that up by hand is slow and error-prone.

    What I built: a Playwright/TypeScript tool that creates test users through the Chargebee API in any of 12+ subscription types (AI Suite, Max Pro, Music & SFX, Teams, Voiceover and more), plus an audit-and-fix run that finds accounts that drifted from their intended state and repairs them. Kept the canonical list of 240 QA accounts mapped to plan and state.

    Result: any subscription scenario reproducible in minutes, by anyone on the team.

    • Playwright
    • TypeScript
    • Chargebee API

    Described in Yuval Bazilevich's letter.

  • Automation

    Playwright journeys in CI

    Scope: the user states the Growth team owned — guest, free trial, upgrade — across the AI Toolkit and the main site.

Specs orchestrate; page objects hold the detail; a regression fails at the gate, not after release.

  • Test design

    Ticket → gap analysis → Gherkin → Zephyr

    Problem: a plan launch multiplies cases fast — every plan × model × checkout path × pricing-page tab — and the gaps hide in the spec, not the code.

    What I did: set up a pipeline from a Jira ticket to a gap analysis against the spec, to Gherkin cases in CSV, imported into Zephyr Scale. Used it to write 44 test plans and coverage matrices (700+ rows): the Unlimited Generations launch, AI-agent pricing and user matrices, Studio onboarding and credits UI, checkout and upgrade flows, exploratory charters.

    Result: cases written and prioritised faster, in one format the whole team could run.

Before QA: Tier 2 support operations

Artlist, April 2024 – June 2025. Escalation point for the support floor — subscriptions, payments and technical faults on web, mobile, plugins and AI products. Root cause first, then a report engineering could reproduce. Along the way I changed how the floor itself worked.

  • Routing

    Subject routing for a ~1,000-ticket day

    Problem: after the AI products launched — Voiceover among them — volume reached about a thousand tickets a day on one or two shared inboxes. Agents picked at random, billing cases were reassigned by hand, and Zendesk dropped the assignee after a reply, so the next agent picked up a thread mid-way.

    What I did: initiated a split by subject — the bot asks the customer for a subject, a tag attaches, the ticket lands in the matching inbox. Tier 1 kept the simple tickets; specialists stayed in theirs. Asked the team lead for the rule that you cannot reply without assigning to yourself, and pushed agents to own a ticket A to Z. A long-waiting queue came later on top.

    Result: better answers, faster resolution, and agents proud of their specialty. The SLA target went from 10 hours to 8 in that period.

    • Zendesk triggers & views
    • Bot flows
    • Tags
  • Incident flow

    War room → Zendesk + Jira

    Problem: urgent bugs lived in Slack threads; support, R&D and the ticket queue each had a different picture.

    What I did: helped set clear rules for what is a war room and what is a regular bug, then worked with DevOps on a Slack bot that opened the urgent bug in Jira and as a problem ticket in Zendesk at the same time.

    Result: one incident, one ID, visible to everyone who needed it.

    • Slack bot
    • Zendesk API
    • Jira
  • Voice of the user

    Feature requests into Jira

    Problem: feature requests died in the support queue as tickets to close.

    What I did: helped promote an extension that captured user ideas and feature requests so agents could open them as Jira tickets, and presented the user's voice and the needed improvements to stakeholders.

    Result: Product saw the request instead of a closed ticket.

  • Content & training

    Macros, Help Center, bot answers, training

    What I did: owned the content layer — macros for agents, Help Center articles for self-service, and the classic Zendesk bot's answers flow. Trained Tier 1 agents, including the remote team. Ran support content and training for Motion Array. Was on the team that tested the third-party AI agent when it replaced the classic bot.

    Result: Motion Array's Trustpilot rating went from 3.8 to 4.0 while I ran it.
