What I built at Artlist

I owned QA for Artlist's Growth team — the subscription, billing and upgrade funnels Yuval Bazilevich calls the team's most business-critical areas — combining dependable manual coverage with automation and tooling that made the whole team faster. Each item below is described in a signed letter further down.

  • “Yoav owned QA across our most business-critical areas — the subscription, billing, and upgrade funnels.” — Yuval Bazilevich, Growth Team Lead, Artlist
  • “He brings strong product knowledge across our platforms and a constructive, adaptable working approach.” — Vili Medina, Head of R&D, Growth, Artlist
  • Billing safety net

    Subscription pricing validation tool

    Problem: pricing and plan misconfigurations in a subscription billing system are expensive and easy to miss by eye.

    What I built: a tool that verifies subscription pricing against expected values across plans and rules, so a wrong price is caught as a failing check rather than by a customer.

    Result: billing misconfigurations caught before they reached production.

    Described in Yuval Bazilevich's letter (Growth Team Lead).

  • Automation

    Playwright + CI end-to-end test infrastructure

    Scope: visitor/guest, free-trial and upgrade flows — the user states the Growth team owned.

    What I did: maintained, extended and improved the Playwright end-to-end suites and their CI integration, so regressions are caught at the gate rather than after release.

    Result: dependable automated coverage of the most business-critical funnels.

    Described in Yuval Bazilevich's and Vili Medina's letters.

  • Test data

    On-demand test-account generation

    Problem: subscription bugs depend on account state — trial, expired, upgraded, specific plan — which is slow to reproduce by hand.

    What I built: automated generation of test accounts in the exact subscription state a scenario needs, on demand.

    Result: subscription-specific scenarios reproducible on demand instead of by hand.

    Described in Yuval Bazilevich's letter.

  • Observability

    Conversion-funnel dashboards with automated alerts

    Problem: a broken step in the funnel shows up as a drop in conversion long before anyone files a bug.

    What I built: analytics dashboards with automated alerts on drops in the conversion funnel, so production behaviour becomes a QA signal.

    Result: funnel regressions surface as alerts instead of surprises.

    Described in Yuval Bazilevich's letter.

  • AI-assisted QA

    A skills library the QA team ran on

    Problem: writing and prioritising test cases from specs and tickets is slow, repetitive work, and the knowledge of how to test a given flow stayed in people's heads.

    What I built: 49 agent skills in a dedicated QA monorepo — each one a written procedure an AI assistant executes the same way every time. The Growth cluster encodes the money paths directly: growth-add-plan, growth-add-country-vat, growth-validate, growth-debug-journey, growth-studio-journeys, toolkit-journey-coverage. Alongside them, review skills (review-code, validate-pr, audit) and planning skills that turn a ticket into a test plan.

    Result: a recurring bug class became a repeatable procedure instead of tribal knowledge — and the library was adopted across the team.

    Described in Yuval Bazilevich's and Vili Medina's letters.

  • Manual coverage

    Ownership of the money paths

    Scope: thorough, dependable manual coverage of the subscription, billing and upgrade funnels.

    Why it mattered: thorough, dependable coverage of the money paths — so a wrong price or a broken upgrade is caught before a customer hits it.

    Described in Yuval Bazilevich's letter.
